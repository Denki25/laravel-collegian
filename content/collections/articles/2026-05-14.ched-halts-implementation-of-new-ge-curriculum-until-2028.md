<p>
            The Commission on Higher Education (CHED) has postponed the pilot implementation of the reframed General Education (GE) curriculum to allow for further review and consultation with its stakeholders, CHED Chairperson Shirley Agrupis announced in a press conference on May 13.<br><br>
            Agrupis said the decision was made after an inter-agency meeting attended by representatives from the Department of Education (DepEd), Teacher Education Council (TED), Second Congressional Commission on Education (EDCOM2), and CHED.<br>
            
            <blockquote><p>
            "[We] really presented the collective manifestations from the different sectors and we came to a decision that there will be no reframed GE implementation across all programs this school year." she added.
            </p></blockquote>
            <p>
            Now eyed to undergo pilot implementation in the Academic Year 2028-2029, CHED's proposed curriculum seeks to reduce redundancies between senior high school and college GE courses, lessening the GE curriculum units from 36 to 18. <br><br>
            However, the plan drew concerns among educational sectors and advocacy groups over the possible reduction of humanities and social sciences courses such as ethics, philosophy, literature, arts, and Philippine history.<br><br>
            Stakeholders also raised fears that the proposed changes could weaken holistic education and result in faculty displacement in higher education institutions.<br><br>
            Agrupis clarified that the proposal does not aim to remove essential learning areas but rather to streamline the curriculum and align it with current workforce demands.   <br>
            
            <blockquote><p>
            “The main goal of this proposed reframing is to review and eliminate the redundancies between the senior high school and college GE courses,” she clarified.
            </p></blockquote>
            <p>

            Meanwhile, the chairperson also acknowledged that the curriculum revisions and innovations may still lead to job displacement among educators. <br><br>
            “CHED is very much aware of that. If there are revisions, if there are innovations, especially in the curriculum, definitely, there will be displacement,” Agrupis stated.<br><br>
            She further emphasized that CHED has already started gathering data from higher education institutions regarding the possible effects of the proposed curriculum changes, even before concerns on faculty displacement gained public attention. <br><br> 
            Agrupis also noted that the commission has established an online feedback system to gather recommendations and comments from stakeholders as consultation continues.<br><br>
            </p>
