      -
        type: text
        text: 'On July 9, those weaknesses were laid bare as multiple hazards struck the Philippines. Typhoon Inday, which intensified the Southwest Monsoon (Habagat), brought heavy rains across parts of Luzon, a magnitude 4.5 earthquake shook Benguet, and Mount Kanlaon produced a moderately explosive eruption in the Visayas. While such a convergence is uncommon, it showed how quickly an ordinary day can turn into a crisis.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: "According to the National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC), Typhoon Inday and the enhanced Habagat affected over 112,500 families and claimed 17 lives throughout the country, while the Benguet earthquake triggered evacuations across the province in Luzon and Mount Kanlaon's eruption blanketed 10 barangays in ash in Negros Oriental in Visayas. "
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'Reading these reports made me realize that the natural disasters on July 9 was not an isolated case, but a fragment of a growing pattern of extreme heat, El Niño-induced water shortages, and widespread flooding that leaves Filipinos with little time to recover before the next disaster strikes.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'The consequences are especially evident in agriculture, where Typhoon Inday alone caused more than P12.5M in crop losses affecting over 1,000 farmers according to the Department of Agriculture (DA), while it also recorded P57.78B in agricultural production losses during the 2024 El Niño, tropical cyclones and pests outbreaks affecting at least 1.4 million farmers and fisherfolk in the country. '
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: '‎Instead of asking why these losses continue to happen, my Facebook feed is often filled with photos and videos of Filipinos wading through floodwaters or helping one another, with the comment sections praising their resilience despite the devastation.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: '‎That spirit is admirable because it reflects the compassion and solidarity that define many Filipino communities. Still, every story of resilience makes me wonder why we continue to celebrate survival instead of demanding the long-term solutions that could have prevented so much suffering in the first place.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: '‎These problems are not difficult to see. In Pampanga, where I grew up, drainage systems are rarely cleaned, and a single heavy downpour is enough to flood entire streets. The same thought comes to mind whenever disasters strike our agricultural communities, where farmers are often the first to lose what they spent months working for.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'What should have been a season of harvest too often becomes another cycle of loss and uncertainty, reminding me that every disaster threatens not only crops but also the livelihoods of those who feed the nation.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'These are not isolated losses but recurring consequences of the same gaps in disaster preparedness. Protecting agriculture should not begin after crops are destroyed but long before disasters strike through sustained investments in climate adaptation, accessible crop insurance, and timely government support that helps farmers recover and prepare for the next season. '
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'These problems persist because early warning systems are often delayed while long-term planning remains inadequate. Just as agriculture requires protection before crops are lost, communities deserve sustained government investment before the next disaster exposes the same failure again. '
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'This July, we once again observe National Disaster Resilience Month under Executive Order No. 29 (s. 2017), which promotes disaster awareness, preparedness, and resilience across the country. As a student, I have experienced this commitment through the National Service Training Program (NSTP), particularly the Civic Welfare Training Service (CWTS), where we were taught first aid, disaster response, community preparedness, and earthquake and evacuation drills. '

        text: 'Yet every disaster reinforces a lesson that preparedness is universal and extends beyond classrooms, especially when many communities still face clogged drainage systems, inadequate flood control, unreliable early warning systems, and infrastructure that cannot withstand disasters.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'Every disaster leaves behind the same lesson, yet we continue to relearn it. It requires sustained collaboration among government, schools, and communities through infrastructure maintenance, and continuous public education so that preparedness becomes part of everyday life rather than a yearly reminder.'
  -
    type: paragraph
    content:
      -
        type: text
        text: 'More importantly, it requires a government that invests in long-term solutions before disasters strike, through stronger flood control, resilient infrastructure, reliable early warning systems, climate adaptation, and sustained support for agriculture.'
