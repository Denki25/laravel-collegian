<p>
            Committee on Agriculture, Food, and Agrarian Reform Chairman Sen. Francis “Kiko” Pangilinan called for an urgent harmonization of local onion production data, following discrepancies between the report of Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and Department of Agriculture (DA), during the Senate Hearing held at DA – Philippine Carabao Center National Headquarters and Genepool, March 26.<br><br>
            The call stemmed from conflicting figures reported by the two agencies on local onion surplus data where the PSA cited a 13,867 metric tons (MT) surplus, while the DA reported a deficit of 22,035 MT, thus tolerating importation of onion which reached up to around 94,000 MT last year.<br><br>
            “Gusto ko, gumawa kayo [PSA and DA] ng Memorandum of Agreement at magkasundo kayo sa pag-harmonize ng inyong mga computation ng harvest, yield, projected consumption, etc. Isa-submit niyo [ang draft] sa Senate Committee on Agriculture. At ‘pag hindi niyo nagawa ‘yan, hindi ko ipapa-apruba ang inyong mga [2027] budget,” Pangilinan said.<br><br>
            Meanwhile, committee member and Senate President Pro Tempore Panfilo “Ping” Lacson stressed the importance of harmonizing data from both agencies, stating that the derived data could be a basis for remedial measures to be implemented in solving the supply concerns.<br><br>
            “Kasi without numbers, we cannot solve anything,” Lacson explained.<br><br>
            Rommel Calingasan, a municipal agriculturist from Occidental Mindoro, shared the same sentiment, saying that the lack of concrete data also delays the formulation of plans and programs to help the local farmers.<br>
            <blockquote><p>
            “Ito po ang hiling ng amin pong municipal agriculturists at magsasaka, na sana madaliing i-harmonize ang atin pong data. ‘Pag nag-import po ang gobyerno, ‘yong data na isina-submit ng mga MAO (Municipal Agriculture Office) sa DA ay hindi nagiging basehan, kasi ang nagiging basehan ay ang data ng PSA,” Calingasan added.
            </p></blockquote>
            <p>
            According to PSA, a harmonization meeting regarding production was held last 2023 with DA and other agencies to point out differences in survey methodologies, concepts, and reference periods.<br><br>
            The signed Memorandum of Agreement is expected to be submitted to the CAFAR before the hearing for the 2027 budget.<br><br>
            Yesterday’s Senate hearing centered on Senate Resolution No. 344, authored by Sen. Loren Legarda, which investigates the systemic collapse of onion farmgate prices. <br><br>
            The resolution alleges that the crisis was triggered by anomalous imports timed to coincide with the local harvest, citing a shortcoming by related agencies to adhere to import calibration and cold storage guidelines.<br><br>
            A clear instance of this incident was cited on SR No. 334 wherein the entry of over 4,000 metric tons of imported onions coincided with the peak local harvest in Nueva Ecija and Occidental Mindoro, resulting to the plummet of onion farmgate prices from P120 – P150 per kilogram in January to P30 – P40 per kilogram in February.
